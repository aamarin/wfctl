**BLOCKER  docs/references/README.md:L71 — fabricated quotation, misattributed to the OpenAI paper.**
The row quotes: *"When people are asked to approve too many actions without
much bandwidth for doing so, the human may start rubber stamping the AI
system's actions" — reached without citing Anthropic*. I extracted the full
text of `practices-for-governing-agentic-ai-systems.pdf` (1181 lines via
`pdftotext -layout`) and grepped for every substring of this sentence
(`rubber stamp`, `bandwidth`, `approve too many`, `too many actions`, `start
rubber`, `may start`). The word "rubber stamp" occurs exactly once in the
entire document, in a completely different sentence, framed as an open
research question, not a stated finding:

> "What are the best practices for users reviewing approvals for high-cost
> actions (such as minimum review times) to avoid their turning into a
> 'rubber stamp' for agentic AI systems that cannot catch harmful actions?"

The word "bandwidth" does not appear anywhere in the document. The quoted
sentence in the record — with its specific framing ("too many actions",
"without much bandwidth", "the human may start rubber stamping ... actions")
— does not exist in this source in any form. This is presented in quotation
marks as a second, independent vendor's verbatim confirmation of the
Anthropic 93%-approval finding; that is the row's entire argumentative
purpose ("reached without citing Anthropic"). The fabrication directly
undermines the one claim in the whole table doing cross-vendor corroboration
work — and the row's own refutation column compounds it (see next finding).
→ Fix: replace with an accurate quote from this document (the real "rubber
stamp" sentence is much weaker — an open question about high-cost-action
review fatigue, not a general finding about approval volume) or drop the row.

**BLOCKER  docs/references/README.md:L71 — refutation condition admits it is not checkable here, contradicting the section's own preamble.**

**BLOCKER  docs/references/README.md:L70 — self-sealing refutation condition (finding contrary evidence is redefined as confirming the row).**
The row's refutation column: *"An agent-initiated merge or force-push in this
repo's reflog. #100 and #101 already hold merge authority out of the loop, so
the local rate is lower than 0.8% rather than higher — which strengthens the
row instead of refuting it."* Read this straight: the stated check is "does an
agent-initiated merge/force-push exist in the reflog." If one were found, that
would be direct evidence *against* "#100 and #101 already hold merge authority
out of the loop" and against the row's use of the 0.8% figure to justify a
narrow row-3 gate — i.e., it would refute the row. Instead the sentence
pre-declares that finding it would "strengthen" the row. A condition that
counts as support regardless of which way the evidence comes out is exactly
the unfalsifiable shape the preamble is written to exclude. (Separately: 0.8%
is a global, cross-organization statistic from Anthropic's usage data: no
amount of single-repo reflog evidence, in either direction, actually moves
that number — which is the more defensible reason this row can't be locally
refuted, but it isn't the reason given.) → Fix: either state a condition that
would genuinely count against the row (e.g., "an agent successfully bypassing
#100/#101's gate" without pre-deciding what it would mean), or admit — as
row 71 does but shouldn't — that this row isn't locally checkable, and cut it.

**WARNING  docs/references/README.md:L75 — refutation condition is stated backwards (it describes confirmation, not refutation).**
Claim: *"A pre-commit hook is fast local pre-catching and not the gate, because
`--no-verify` skips it. The gate is whatever runs where the actor is not."*
Refutation: *"A wfctl check running where the agent has no write access — CI,
or branch protection on the tracker."* But a check running where the agent has
no write access is a positive instance of the claim ("the gate is whatever
runs where the actor is not"), not a counterexample to it. As written, finding
the described thing would confirm the row, not refute it. This is the same
inversion as the L70 finding above, in a smaller way. Also note the AWS page
itself (fetched and read in full) never mentions `--no-verify` or frames
pre-commit hooks as non-authoritative — it recommends layering pre-commit,
IDE, and CI checks together without ranking one as "not the gate." That
specific reasoning is the record author's own inference, not something DL.LD.5
argues; presenting it beside the citation without quotation marks is
technically honest (the "claim taken" column isn't in quotes here, unlike most
rows) but a reader skimming the table will read it as what the source says.

**WARNING  docs/references/README.md:L76 — quotation altered by inserting a word not in the source.**
The row quotes: *"An untrusted server can claim `readOnlyHint: true` and
delete your files anyway."* I fetched the article and had it reproduce the
exact sentence: the real text is *"A server can claim `readOnlyHint: true`
and delete your files anyway."* — with no "untrusted." The word "untrusted"
appears in the *next* sentence of the source ("This is why the spec says
clients must treat annotations from untrusted servers as untrusted"), not in
the sentence being quoted. Inserting it into the quotation changes what's
being claimed: the original makes the stronger, unqualified point that *any*
server's hint is unreliable; the altered quote narrows it to untrusted
servers, which is a different (weaker, question-begging) claim and reads as
though the source itself hedged where it didn't. → Fix: quote the actual
sentence verbatim.

**WARNING  docs/references/README.md:L72, L77 — two rows cite sources with no link, unlike every other row.**
"Bravo-Lillo et al., SOUPS 2014" and "Orseau and Armstrong, *Safely
Interruptible Agents* (UAI 2016)" are the only two entries in the ten-row
table with no URL. Both papers are real (confirmed by search — Bravo-Lillo's
actual SOUPS 2014 paper is "Harder to Ignore? Revisiting Pop-Up Fatigue and
Approaches to Prevent It," USENIX-hosted PDF available; Orseau & Armstrong's
paper is on the ACM DL and elsewhere), so this isn't fabrication, but the
inconsistency is unexplained and reduces verifiability against the file's own
"nothing here binds anything, but everything here should be checkable"
posture. More substantively for L72: the specific figures attributed to it —
"50–70% click-through," "about half dismissed inside two seconds,"
"habituation is measurable after two or three exposures" — do not appear to
be Bravo-Lillo's numbers at all. The 50–70%-range click-through figure and
the "seconds to dismiss" finding trace to a different pair of papers in the
same literature (Sunshine et al., "Crying Wolf," USENIX Security 2009, and
Akhawe & Felt, "Alice in Warningland," USENIX Security 2013 — the latter
reports 70.2% Chrome / ~33% Firefox SSL-warning click-through), and
Bravo-Lillo's own habituation methodology used far more exposures (22, or
2.5 minutes) than "two or three." The row's phrase "and the browser-warning
literature it sits in" is doing the work of covering this gap, but the effect
is that a reader who clicks through expecting to verify "50–70%" and "two
seconds" against Bravo-Lillo's paper won't find them there. → Fix: cite
Sunshine et al. and/or Akhawe & Felt by name for the specific numbers, and
keep Bravo-Lillo (with its own URL) for the habituation-methodology point it
actually supports.

**NIT  docs/references/README.md:L74 — NIST citation goes through a third-party mirror rather than an authoritative source.**
`csf.tools` is a commercial third party's copy of NIST SP 800-53, not
`csrc.nist.gov` or `nvd.nist.gov`. The content I fetched was accurate to
AC-3(3)'s substance, so this isn't wrong, just a weaker citation than the
project would presumably want for a control catalog that has an official,
stable URL.

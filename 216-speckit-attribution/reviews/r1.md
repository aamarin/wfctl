Thirteen of fifteen behave as the docstrings say. The regexes match what they
claim: `_ROW`'s widened class `[a-z0-9./-]` picks up both tables and nothing
else in the record (the two header rows have prose before the first backtick, so
they cannot match), and `_TEMPLATE_ROW` partitions them cleanly. Re-spelling a
template row relative to `specify/` instead of `wfctl/` would push it into
`_listed()` and fail loudly rather than silently — the right direction.
`_noticed_templates()`'s section bound is likewise fail-safe: break the
`\n    Copyright` split (M14) and `templates/github-issue-template.md` leaks
into the set and fails a test, rather than the set quietly emptying.

**WARNING — M4 and M15 are the gap.** For skills and scripts, the declaration
lives *in the file*, so a rename changes the key and both directions fire. For
templates the two declarations are both documents, and neither
`_listed_templates()` nor `_noticed_templates()` ever stats the tree. A derived
template deleted or renamed leaves the record and the shipped notice both
asserting GitHub's copyright over a file that is no longer there, with a green
suite. This is not the acknowledged #213 hole (an *arrival* declaring nothing);
it is a departure that both declarations keep claiming, and it is two lines to
close:

```python
missing_on_disk = sorted(p for p in _listed_templates()
                         if not (SPECIFY_ROOT.parent / p).exists())
assert not missing_on_disk, ...
```

Worth noting because `test_every_template_the_record_lists_is_named_in_the_specify_notice`'s
docstring says it is "checked in the direction a rename breaks". It is checked
in the direction a rename *of the notice entry* breaks. A rename of the file
breaks nothing.

Two smaller correctness notes, both benign:

- The scripts' entries in the specify notice's file list are derived data that
  nothing checks (only `templates/…` names are read back). A script removed from
  the record and from disk but left in that paragraph drifts unnoticed. Low
  stakes — the scripts' own lines are the authoritative declaration — but it is
  the same shape of hand-kept list the record elsewhere refuses.
- `test_every_upstream_named_in_a_file_has_its_notice_in_the_same_tree` routes on
  `name.startswith("specify/")`. Safe today (skill keys are bare directory
  names), and it would take a skill directory literally named `specify` to

## 3. Architecture

**The placement of `wfctl/specify/templates/NOTICES.md` is sound**, and I
checked the mechanism rather than the prose. `_RUNTIME_TARGETS` is
`[("specify/scripts", ".specify/scripts"), ("specify/templates",
".specify/templates")]` and the install loop iterates `src.iterdir()` with no
extension filter, so the notice is copied into a project's
`.specify/templates/NOTICES.md`. Nothing globs that directory: every consumer
reads a named file (`spec-template.md`, `plan-template.md`, `tasks-template.md`,
`checklist-template.md`, `constitution-template.md`, `agent-file-template.md`),
so an extra `NOTICES.md` cannot be mistaken for a template. `create-new-feature.sh`
`cp`s one named file, not the directory, so the notice cannot leak into a
generated `spec.md`. Full suite in the scratch copy: **942 passed**;
`ruff check wfctl/ tests/`: clean. (`CLAUDE.md` still says 653 tests — pre-existing
drift, not this diff's problem.)

**WARNING — the record now implies a completeness it does not have.** This diff
deletes the paragraph that said "The table is not yet the whole answer… absence
from this table means 'not yet recorded' for those and 'wfctl's own' for
everything else", and replaces it with a positive claim that the eight skills
"were identified by content". But `wfctl/agents/commands/speckit.*.md` is inside
the same `graft wfctl/agents`, ships in every wheel, *and* installs into
`.agents/commands/` and `.claude/commands/` — and eight of those wrappers are
substantially upstream text. Measured the same way #216 measured everything
else (fraction of substantive lines appearing verbatim in the upstream
counterpart at `templates/commands/<name>.md`):

```
speckit.tasks.md         14/17   speckit.clarify.md        9/12
speckit.plan.md          13/16   speckit.constitution.md   8/12
speckit.specify.md        9/12   speckit.analyze.md        5/8
speckit.checklist.md      5/8    speckit.implement.md      5/8
```

The identical parts are upstream's `description:` string, upstream's `handoffs:`
block verbatim (labels, prompts, `send: true`), and the whole `## User Input` /
`$ARGUMENTS` / "You **MUST** consider the user input before proceeding (if not
empty)." stanza. `speckit.brainstorm/brief/decompose/orchestrate` have no
upstream counterpart (404) and are genuinely wfctl's own.

Two things make this worth raising rather than waving through as out of scope.
First, the record's own Considered section forecloses the natural defence: "Sort
the derived skills by how far they have drifted and attribute only the close
ones — drift is evidence of derivation, not a threshold for it." A 13-of-16
wrapper cannot be too small to matter under a record that says 20% is enough.
Second, #213's stated lesson is that "a record that omits a known exception is
read as denying it" — and the sentence carrying that exception is what this diff
removes. #218 does not cover them (it is about unvendored sibling files).

I would not expand this diff to attribute twelve more files. The cheap fix is
one sentence in the record — either "the `speckit.*` command wrappers under
`wfctl/agents/commands/` are the remaining unassessed spec-kit surface (#NNN)"
with a filed issue, or a stated finding that they were assessed and why they are
excluded. Anything is better than the silence that #213 named as the failure.

**NIT — the "must reach a project" standard is applied to one tree only.** The
record's new paragraph and `test_every_derived_template_that_ships_reaches_a_project`
make reachability load-bearing for `specify/`, while `wfctl/agents/NOTICES.md`
sits at the top of its graft and reaches no project (confirmed: `_BASE_TARGETS`
is `agents/skills` and `agents/commands`; a top-level file in the tree belongs
to no layer — the test's docstring says so). The distinction is defensible — an
installed skill carries its own line, an installed template carries nothing — and
the record makes exactly that argument for the scripts ("their line travels
inside them") but never extends it to `agents/NOTICES.md`. One sentence closes
it. Without it, the asymmetry reads as an oversight, and the obvious next move
(mirror the specify placement by moving the notice to `agents/skills/`) looks
right when it may not be.

**NIT — one fact, three homes.** The "why `templates/` and not the tree root"
argument is now in the record (L113-119), in the notice itself (L15-18), and in
a comment in the test (L40-44). `knowledge-placement` is invoked two paragraphs
away to justify the line/row pair on the grounds that they are *different*
facts; these three are the same fact. The record is the home. The consumer
reading `.specify/templates/NOTICES.md` does not need wfctl's directory-layout
reasoning, and nothing checks the three copies against each other.

## 4. Readability

Comment and docstring style matches the repository: every new comment explains
why this shape, and every test docstring names the failure it exists to catch,
per `CLAUDE.md`. Test names are sentences. `NO_COLOR` is not relevant here (no
console output). Names are accurate with one exception:
`test_every_derived_template_that_ships_reaches_a_project` asserts nothing about
templates — it asserts where the notice is. The docstring is explicit about
that, so the name is a wart, not a defect.

**NIT** — `wfctl/specify/templates/NOTICES.md:L3` opens "wfctl is MIT-licensed;
see `LICENSE` at the root of the distribution", and L58 closes with "wfctl's own
copyright as stated in `LICENSE`". Four paragraphs down the same file says "This
file travels into projects wfctl installs" — and in an installed project,
`LICENSE` at the root is the *project's* licence, which may be anything or
absent. The file is the one artifact in this change whose whole job is being
legally unambiguous to a stranger. "wfctl's own `LICENSE`, shipped in the wfctl
package" costs three words.

**NIT** — L191 of the record is 90 columns in a file otherwise wrapped at ~79;
it is the only added line over 82. Reflow.

**NIT** — `metadata.author: 'github-spec-kit'` and `metadata.source:` survive in
seven of the eight skills. The record explains at length why the `license:` key
was abandoned as an identifier, but says nothing about these, which now sit
beside the authoritative line as an unchecked second claim about the same file.
Either they are dead weight to delete or they deserve the one sentence the
`license:` key got.

## 5. Performance

Nothing. The suite reads ~40 small files; the two record parses (NIT above) cost
microseconds and are a tidiness point, not a performance one. No runtime code
path changed — the scripts gain one comment line each, parsed once per
invocation.

## 6. Over-engineering

The diff is close to minimal for what it has to do. Three small `shrink:`
candidates and nothing to `delete:`:

- `tests/test_skill_attribution.py:L82-98` — `_listed()` and
  `_listed_templates()` each read and re-parse `RECORD`; one `_rows()` helper
  with two filters is ~4 lines shorter and removes the chance of the two
  drifting on a pattern change.
- `tests/test_skill_attribution.py:L196` — `assert SPECIFY_NOTICES.exists()` is
  strictly redundant (three other tests already `read_text()` it and would raise),
  but it buys a readable message over a `FileNotFoundError`. Keep it; the rubric
  says a single self-check is the minimum, not bloat.
- `wfctl/specify/templates/NOTICES.md:L15-18` — the paragraph covered by the
  "one fact, three homes" NIT above; −4 lines.

net: −6 lines possible

## Verdict

**Approve.** No blockers. The change does what #216 asks, the mechanism is
sound, the placement decision is right for a reason I could verify in
`_RUNTIME_TARGETS` rather than only read in prose, and the new checks genuinely
fail on thirteen of fifteen mutations — which is a far better ratio than the
average "the tests pass" claim. The two WARNINGs should be fixed here or
deferred with a filed follow-up: the unlisted command wrappers (because this
diff removes the caveat that covered them) and the templates' unstat'd rows
(because two lines close it).
